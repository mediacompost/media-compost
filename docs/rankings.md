# Rankings and batch sessions

Three full-window **sessions** put the library's pictures in front of you: **Rate items** asks which of two pictures is better on some axis, **Tag items one by one** asks whether a picture carries a tag, one picture and one key at a time, and **Tag items in a grid** shows a whole batch pre-sorted into three bands for the mouse to correct. All three live behind the grid's quick-actions button (the lightning bolt beside the media-kind control, which also lists the [T and Q overlays](library.md#quick-tag-from-the-keyboard-t)), and none has a shortcut of its own. **Rate items** is also a ranking row's own menu entry in the library sidebar, which starts straight in on that axis where the button always opens the chooser.

All three take the **current view** as their pool — the search, the category and the media-kind filter as the grid shows them — and a selection is *priority*, never a fence: selected pictures come first, and the rest of the view follows.

## Rankings

A **ranking** places pictures on an axis — quality, composition, art style — by quick side-by-side comparisons. It has a name and a **rating scale** (0–9 by default, anywhere within 0–100): the numbers the axis counts in, with the best picture at the top of the range and the worst at the bottom, however many there are.

**A ranking tags nothing.** It used to own a tag namespace and write a score tag onto every picture it placed; putting something on the pictures is now the **Assign ratings** action's business (the grid's quick-actions menu), which writes ordinary tags you asked for by rules you set. What a ranking holds is the comparisons and the standings fitted from them.

A ranking is met in the **library sidebar**: a **Rankings** row between Ungrouped and Hidden, shown once the library has one, expanding to a row per ranking (and to its pools, past one).

**The Rankings row is a view of its own** — a breadcrumb root, like Trash and Hidden — and what it shows is a **card per ranking**, drawn the way a sequence's card is: a stack shadow, a mosaic of its four best pictures, a badge in the corner, and under it the comparisons and the pools. A ranking is that kind of thing on this screen: a container you open. Nothing there is selected and no item is listed, so the right-hand panel says what the view is rather than offering actions over pictures that are not on screen. The row's own menu holds the one verb that is about no ranking in particular — **New ranking…** — and the new ranking is opened, where "nothing placed yet" is the thing to say.

Picking a ranking makes the **grid** that ranking — the pictures it has placed, best first, in sections by the standings' own buckets ("Score 9", "Score 8", …), with **Jump to** listing every one of them and its count. The sort control stands down and reads *Ranking order*, and the grouping beside it reads *Rating*. The right-hand panel says what the ranking IS when nothing is selected — its scale, its pools, their comparisons, and a caveat where those comparisons cannot yet put its pictures in one order.

**Three verbs about pictures, offered at three sizes**: *Not applicable* (reversible — the picture leaves the fit, its comparisons are kept), *Rate it after all*, and *Remove from this ranking* (deletes its comparisons and refits, which is the one thing here [History](history.md) cannot undo). They act on the **picked picture** in the Ranking tab, on a whole **selection** in that same tab, and on **everything in the view** from the panel with nothing selected.

A ranking row's own menu holds **Rate on this ranking**, **Assign ratings**, **Edit…** and **Delete**, which names how many comparisons go with it. (Until 2026-09 this was a table on a **Rankings** sub-tab of the Tags tab, with a histogram of thumbnails folded out of each row.)

**The overview is the grid itself.** The buckets are the sections a ranking's view arrives grouped into — the pictures themselves, at the size you judge them, under headings you can scroll and jump between — which is what the old rankings page's histogram of 54-pixel thumbnails was trying to be.

**The judgements are the record, and the standings are read off them.** Every time they are asked for they are fitted from every judgement (a Bradley–Terry fit, with a prior so one win is not certainty) and the sorted pool is cut into the buckets — the best picture always reads the top bucket, and pictures whose only evidence is a tie share one. Nothing is stored, so nothing can go stale, and there is no refresh to run. Undo goes through the judgements: each one is its own History entry and reverts one by one.

A pool whose comparisons cannot put its pictures in one order yet is marked **(Too few comparisons)** in the panel: the standings are drawn either way — that is how you watch the evidence come in — but they claim more than the answers support.

### Pools

A ranking is computed over one or more **pools** — populations with standings of their own under the one rating scale, so *illustrations* and *photographs* can be rated on the same "Quality" axis without competing with each other. A ranking always has at least one pool; the first is made with it and unnamed (shown as *Default*), and the last one cannot be removed. A picture joins a pool by being rated in it — there is nothing to add it to, and the same picture may be in several pools of one ranking.

The standings are fitted **per pool** on the one scale: a picture rated in two pools stands at 5 in one and 7 in the other, and the Ranking tab shows a row per pool it is placed in. *Not applicable* applies to the whole ranking.

The ranking's edit dialog holds the **Pools** list — rename the default, add more, drag a row's grip to reorder them, remove one (removing a pool deletes the comparisons made in it and cannot be undone — Save turns red and asks once before it does; adding and renaming revert like any edit). In the sidebar a ranking with several pools expands to a row each, and picking one scopes the grid to that pool's standings.

## Rate items

**Rate items…** opens a chooser: tick the rankings to judge (the click order is the order — a pair is answered on each axis in turn before the next pair comes up, so finding a pair worth comparing is paid once for several answers), pick whether to ask about **images** or **videos** (a film in a run of stills is a stall), and press **Start**. A ticked ranking with more than one [pool](#pools) unfolds a row per pool to tick — with one pool nothing is shown — and each judgement is written to every pool picked for that ranking.

The rankings are **made and edited from the list itself**: **New ranking…** is its last row, and hovering a ranking shows a pencil. Both open the same dialog the sidebar's own menus open, in place of the chooser rather than on top of it; closing it brings the chooser back. A ranking made here is ticked and asked **last**, joining the axes already picked rather than replacing them. The context menu's **Rate on ‹ranking›** rows skip the chooser and start on that one axis, with the view's own media filter; a ranking with several pools opens the chooser with that ranking ticked instead, since which pool the judgements go to is a question.

A **Settings** button in the session header reopens that chooser over the run, the session's own axes and kind ticked; **Continue** takes over from the next pair, keeping the session's answers and its undo.

The session shows two pictures at a time — a sequence gets a pager over its pages, a video plays muted with its controls — and takes one answer per pair from the keyboard:

| Key | Does |
| --- | --- |
| `←` / `→` (or a click) | The left / right picture is better |
| `↓` | A tie — half a win each, and an answer that counts |
| `Space` | Skip — records nothing; the pair does not come back this session |
| `⇧ ←` / `⇧ →` | Ticks that side's **Not applicable**: the picture leaves this axis. The pair stays until both sides are ticked, then moves on; a judgement is refused while either is ticked |
| `↑` | Undo the previous answer (a mark included) and put the pair back |
| `Tab` | The **standings** — the pool best-first, grouped by bucket — and back |
| `Esc` | End the session (after asking, once anything has been answered) |

Which ranking is being asked sits between the pictures with a pip per axis (and the pools the answer goes to, where the ranking has several); the header counts the pairs answered this session and the items in the pool. A card zooms with the wheel and can be turned with its hover buttons, so a sideways scan is rated rather than skipped. Ending the session writes nothing and refreshes nothing — the standings were already the answers you gave.

## Assign ratings

A ranking tags nothing by itself. **Assign ratings…**, in the grid's quick-actions menu under the three sessions, is what spends its standings: it learns the ranking's own numbers from the pictures it has placed, carries them to the ones it has not in the same embedding spaces the tag sessions order by, and writes the tags you ask for wherever a picture lands. It is not a session — an ordinary dialog that makes one bulk write, with a live preview under it.

**The rules are the rating scale cut into bands.** Each row is a **range on the left and the tags it writes beside it**, and the card runs **highest band first** — so a row reads across as one sentence and the card reads down as the scale itself. A row says where its band *starts*; the next number up is where it ends, and the topmost has no top. The bands therefore partition the scale and every picture falls in exactly one.

A fresh dialog opens on **one row per bucket**. Editing a number slides its row to where it belongs; the row's **✚** cuts its band in two (half way along), and the **✕** beside it takes the row out, its neighbour above widening to cover what it held. What a band writes is the quick-assign editor — the same chips with their +/− and the same one adder for tags and groups — and a band with no tags in it writes nothing and is simply a boundary. Each row says how many pictures it takes and shows the highest of them with their numbers; pressing one opens the preview over the dialog. **The rules are remembered per ranking**, in this browser.

**Estimate a score for unrated pictures** is the one option. The pictures the ranking has *placed* are always covered, each at the standing you gave it — never at the fit's guess about it, which would be near that standing rather than it. What the switch turns on is the rest.

Under twelve rated pictures the dialog refuses rather than guesses. **Write the tags** queues a background task rather than holding the dialog open: the scope can be the whole library, the task list carries its progress and its cancel, and what a cancel finds already written stays. Everything it writes is an ordinary, revertible assignment.

## Tag items one by one

**Tag items one by one…** opens its chooser: the tags to decide, in **groups**, whether to **ask about images or videos**, and **Smart ordering** with the embedder it reads. Tag presets remember a set by its tags, and **Skip pictures in sequences** leaves a book's pages and a GIF's frames out (the rate session's chooser has the same switch). A **Settings** button in the session header reopens the chooser mid-run; **Continue** picks up from the picture on screen. The number box beside each tag is a menu: pick the digit it answers to (several tags may share one, and the digit then files or toggles them together; a chip click in the session is about one tag). The tag field decides the **mode**:

- **One tag** — a yes/no session: `→` or `Y` assigns it, `←` or `N` is "doesn't fit" — the tag's counter tag, its group's negative, or (a bare tag) nothing.
- **Several tags** — a toggling session: digits light the tags that apply, `↓` commits the picture as it stands, `0` commits it with nothing lit.

**The set is questions.** Every row belongs to a question and there is always at least one (**Add question** in the section header adds another; **Clear** empties the set to one empty question). Each question has its own two switches: **Mutually exclusive** — at most one of its tags is lit on a picture, and lighting another puts the lit one out — and **Assign negative tags** — its unlit tags are written negatively when the picture is committed (a real "does not apply", which keeps the picture from being offered again and teaches the ordering); with that switch off, an unlit tag still teaches the session's own ordering and the library is simply never told. A question with nothing in it has no effect. With that switch on, each row shows the tag twice — the tag itself behind a green box, and after a slash what "doesn't fit" writes: the same tag behind a red box, struck through, by default. Click either name to edit it in place: renaming the tag carries the default negative along, while typing another name on the negative side makes it a **counter tag** — assigned positively instead of the negative (`low_quality` beside `high_quality`, the grid session's rule per tag), shown behind a green box and no longer following the tag; empty that field to get the negative back. **A row may be a group from the library rather than a tag**: it is answered exactly like one and what it writes is a membership, so it has no negative form — "not in that group" is not something to record — and it is offered no counter tag; a session still needs at least one real tag, since the pool and the ordering are about tags. Rows drag by their grip — a row within its question or into another, a question among the questions. The digit keys belong to rows, never to questions. **Tag presets** remember the set alone — the questions with their switches, their rows with counter tags and digits — and loading one leaves the session's other settings (which kind, the ordering, the sequence rule) as they are.

Everywhere: `Space` skips (nothing is written), `↓` commits what is on screen — negatives and counter tags included — `↑` reverts the previous answer whole (a "yes" to a pending machine guess resolves it through the ordinary approve path, so its every consequence comes back), `T` opens the [quick tag](library.md#quick-tag-from-the-keyboard-t) field over the picture on screen, `I` shows the picture's details, `Tab` toggles the summary (the header's Summary button is the same toggle and stays lit while it is up — the rate session's Standings button works the same way), and `Esc` ends the session after one question (skipped when nothing was answered), leaving the summary — whose thumbnails **reopen** an item — a wrong answer is corrected without undoing everything since.

Every write is an ordinary tag assignment, logged and revertible like any other; the pool leaves out pictures already decided for the tags in the set, while a machine's *pending* guess stays in — deciding it is the most valuable answer.

**Smart ordering** is a classifier that learns from the session's own answers: with pictures indexed by an embedder (the import overlay's *Index for smart ordering*, or the chooser's own **Index remaining** button, which runs a background task), the queue puts likely matches first and keeps sampling near the decision boundary, so a "yes" run stays a "yes" run. Unindexed pictures still come, after the ordered ones. The row explains itself when it cannot work — no embedder set up (a **Set up** button leads to Settings → Actions), or nothing indexed yet — and the session header says "unordered" when it is on but has nothing to go on.

## Tag items in a grid

**Tag items in a grid…** is the one-by-one session's sibling for the mouse, for pictures only. Its chooser asks for the **question** — one list with a row per tag, and an adder under it that takes another tag or a **group** from the library (a picture that fits joins it, and nothing ever takes it out again) — the **grid** (columns × rows, 4 × 4 by default), whether to **prioritize uncertain pictures**, whether to **skip pictures in sequences**, which **answers** the capsule offers (fits is always one; "doesn't fit" or "undecided" can each be dropped — without "undecided", every card gets a decision and a card the model left undecided starts on "doesn't fit"), whether to **include already tagged pictures** (they stay in the pool, each starting on what it carries rather than on the classifier's guess — the way to check tags already given; Next batch then writes only what was changed, and a picture moved to undecided has its tags taken off), and which embedder sorts — DINOv2, CLIP, or **both fused**, each space fit and scored on its own and a picture's score the mean of the spaces it is indexed in, with a coverage line per space and one **Index remaining** button that queues a run for every space that is short. Sorting cannot be switched off: it is what the session is for.

While "doesn't fit" is offered, each row shows its tag **twice** — the tag behind a green box and, after a slash, what that answer means for it: the same tag in red and struck through by default, or a **counter tag** typed in its place, which is assigned positively instead (`low_quality` beside `high_quality`). Both names are edited in place by clicking them, and emptying one puts the default back — over an empty field the tag sets' tree opens, so it leads with a **Close** row. With a counter tag and a question of **one** tag, **Offer "both"** adds a fourth answer that assigns both tags — for a tag that holds for one subject in the picture and not another; such a picture is decided but teaches the classifier neither side.

**With more than one tag the question is a conjunction, and the two answers stop being symmetric.** A picture **fits** when it carries **every** tag of the set, and it **doesn't fit** when something in the library speaks against **any one** of them — that tag assigned negatively, or the counter paired with it assigned positively. A picture carrying one of two tags is neither: it is undecided, which is exactly the picture the session exists to ask about. "Fits" is written as it always was, every tag of the set assigned. "Doesn't fit" is not, because it says only *not `a` or not `b`* and no assignment can spell that: writing the negative on each tag separately would claim what nobody said — the picture may well be `a`, just not `a` **and** `b`. So with several tags **that answer writes nothing at all**, counter tags included. It still means something — the picture leaves the session's queue, and the answer goes back to the classifier as a label, so the bands go on getting better as the session runs — but it does not outlive the session: the library is never told, and a later session over the same set starts where this one did. Negatives already in the library are untouched by this and still count, whoever wrote them. The one thing the answer still changes is what an earlier one put there: with *include already tagged pictures* on, a card can arrive carrying the whole set, and moving it to "doesn't fit" takes those tags off — a retraction rather than a claim, and the only way such a card can be corrected here. With **one** tag there is no disjunction and nothing changes: "doesn't fit" writes the tag negatively, or its counter positively, exactly as before.

Start fills the grid with a batch already sorted from the set's existing assignments; each cell's **border** says the answer — green fits, red doesn't fit, grey undecided — and a card never moves when its answer changes. Where the answers are cut is calibrated from those assignments rather than fixed, and a side with only a handful of labels does not pre-fill at all, so a fresh session starts entirely undecided. Every batch is refit from what the previous one wrote — and from what it answered without writing — so the sorting improves as the session goes. With **prioritize uncertain pictures** on, half of each batch is what the model is least sure about, which is where it learns fastest; off, a batch is simply the likeliest matches.

A **Settings** button in the session header reopens the chooser over the run; **Continue** takes over from the next batch. Hovering a cell shows an **eye** in its top-left corner, which opens the ordinary [Quick Look](library.md) over the session with its sidebar and its `T` field, a **✕** in the top-right that skips the picture (nothing written, its cell taken by the next one from the queue), and a **capsule** at the bottom with the three answers: **+** fits, **−** doesn't fit, and the slashed tag for undecided; clicking the picture itself cycles through them. **Next batch** writes the borders as they stand — green assigns the set's tags and joins its groups, red assigns the "doesn't fit" spelling of a single tag and writes nothing for a set of several, grey writes nothing — and sorts the next batch; **End** beside it writes the grid and goes straight to the summary; **Undo batch** takes the previous batch back whole.

From the keyboard: nothing is selected until the first arrow press, which lands on the first card; then the arrows walk the grid in two dimensions — `→` / `←` the next and previous card in reading order (off a row's end, the next row's first), `↓` / `↑` the card below and above — `⇧ ←` / `⇧ →` turn the cursor's answer and `⇧⌥ ←` / `⇧⌥ →` turn every card's at once (a batch whose cards agree steps together; one whose cards differ is first put onto the first card's answer), `Space` opens and closes the preview — on the first card when none is selected (with it open the walk is one-dimensional, `↓`/`→` next and `↑`/`←` previous, the preview follows, `⇧ ←`/`⇧ →` still turn the answer, shown in a capsule under the picture, `T` opens the quick tag field over it, and `Enter` closes it — a batch is not written from behind a picture), `T` opens the quick tag field on the cursor's card, `Enter` is Next batch, `U` (or `⌘Z`) is Undo batch, `Tab` toggles the summary — whose thumbnails reopen a single picture, the undecided ones included — and `Esc` ends the session after one question.
